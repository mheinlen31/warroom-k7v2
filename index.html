How the numbers work

Model $ is value over replacement. For each position the model counts every unfilled starting slot in the league (plus a share of FLEX and bench demand), finds the player you'd get for nothing at that depth — the replacement level — and values everyone by projected points above him. The room's spendable money is then shared out in proportion. Scarcity is built in: when the elite backs go and eight rosters still have an empty RB1, the replacement level falls and every remaining RB is worth more.

Mkt $ is ESPN's auction value scaled by live inflation — the money still in the room against the ADP value of the players who'll actually get drafted. It's what the table will pay if it bids on instinct.

Edge is Model minus Mkt: green means the room is likely to underpay him, red that he'll go for more than he's worth to a lineup. Bidders is how many teams can legally roster him and have the max bid to pay his market price — two or fewer and he may go cheap.

Calibrated to this league. Nine years of Sunday Funday auctions (2017–25, keepers excluded) set how the model splits money between positions — about 52% of auction dollars go to RBs, 35% to WRs, 5–6% each to QB and TE, and roughly 1% each to K and D/ST — and shape the spend curve inside each position, so the model knows this room pays one QB and shrugs at the rest. Kickers and defenses are capped at what they've actually gone for: $1, $2 for the best one on the board, $3 only for a true outlier.

Sources. Projections, values and ADP are ESPN's; "cons" is the mean of ESPN's own rankers. Drop extra ranking files (FantasyPros, Yahoo, your own sheet — CSV or XLSX) into sources/ and run prep.sh: consensus becomes the mean across all of them, projections blend in (a rank-only source gets an implied projection from ESPN's rank-to-points curve), and each row shows how many sources agree. 2025 line, bye weeks, experience. Each row carries last season's stats that score for the position (yards, TDs, receptions, INTs; FG/XP; sacks, takeaways, points allowed) with total fantasy points under ESPN's default PPR scoring and games played, plus the team's 2026 bye week. R marks a rookie, 2Y a second-year player; the ROOKIES and 2ND YR tabs list them.

Tiers break on projection gaps within a position; a cliff marks the last player before a big drop. Projections, values and consensus rank are ESPN's; consensus is the mean of their individual rankers and spread is how far those rankers disagree.
